# Understanding Company X (Deep Company Series): Break Down One Company in 3-8 Long-Form Essays

Write an "Understanding X" deep long-form series (3-8 essays, sized to company complexity — see "Sizing to Complexity") for $ARGUMENTS, to be published on public channels (blog, newsletter, Substack, LinkedIn, etc.). **The core IP is not "writing well" but "editing hard" — 99% of finance articles violate this skill's fact-checking standard.**

Reference sample: `reports/{Company}/Understanding-{Company}/`

---

## 1. Trigger Scenario

The user wants a "textbook-grade" deep study of a company, published publicly as a **long-form series**. Distinct from a single research note:
- 3-8 essays (a complex company at 8 essays runs ~120,000 words; a simple company at 3 essays ~30,000 words), forming a complete loop from cognitive reset to decision framework
- Each essay stands alone (good for single-piece sharing), but a shared valuation/management/price judgment runs through all of them
- Written for readers "willing to spend 90 minutes to truly understand a company," not for brokerage clients

**Scenarios where this skill does NOT fit**: a single research note, an earnings comment, industry research — use `/investment-research`, `/earnings-review`, or `/industry-research` for those.

## 2. Series Outline Template

### Sizing Essay Count to Company Complexity (decide count first, then the outline)

**The count is not fixed at 8.** Eight essays is the capacity that only a company with "multiple business lines + a massive investment portfolio + a 20-year management story" (e.g., a large conglomerate or holding company) can sustain. Before writing, ask: how many main axes does this company have that can each stand as an essay around "one sharp question"? Write as many essays as there are axes.

| Complexity | Characteristics | Essays | Example |
|------------|-----------------|--------|---------|
| High | Multiple business lines each a business in itself + hidden assets/investment portfolio + rich management history | 7-8 | A conglomerate/holding co (e.g., Itaúsa-type) |
| Medium | 2-3 business lines + one major era-defining variable | 4-6 | — |
| Low | Clear core business, few and concentrated core questions | **3** | A single-business compounder (e.g., a rental-car or industrial leader) |

**Standard way to merge into 3 essays** (the 8 axes below are still a checklist — all must be covered, just folded into 3 essays):

| # | Essay-Name Direction | Axes Folded In |
|---|----------------------|----------------|
| 01 | You Think You Understand X (opening + business & moat) | Cognitive reset + moat + profit-engine overview |
| 02 | X's Biggest Variable (current narrative / hidden assets) | Hidden assets + AI/era variable |
| 03 | What Price Is Worth Buying, What Signal Forces a Sell (financials + decision finale) | Financial depth + management key points + valuation decision |

Judgment signal: if halfway through an essay you find yourself "padding content" (the same fact reappears in different wording), you set the count too high — merge. Conversely, if an essay exceeds 12,000 words and still can't fit, split it.

### The Full 8-Axis Template (for complex companies; fold as a checklist for simple ones)

| # | Essay-Name Template | Core Question | Word Count |
|---|---------------------|---------------|------------|
| 01 | You Think You Understand X, But You Don't | Cognitive reset: break 3 common illusions | 4,000-5,000 |
| 02 | X's Moat — `<one-sentence business essence>` | How deep is the moat, will it still be there in 5/10 years | 6,000-8,000 |
| 03 | X's Biggest Profit Engine — `<most profitable business>` | What the core business is, why it lasts | 6,000-8,000 |
| 04 | The Other Company Hidden on X's Balance Sheet — `<hidden asset>` | Investment portfolio / subsidiaries / hidden value | 8,000-10,000 |
| 05 | In the AI (or current narrative) Era, Is X a Winner or Loser | Era variable: dissect AI's impact business by business | 8,000-10,000 |
| 06 | Dissecting X's Financials the Buffett Way | Financial depth: gross margin / FCF / ROE / SBC | 8,000-10,000 |
| 07 | `<management quote>` — Is X's Management Worth Trusting | Capital-allocation discipline + integrity test + succession | 8,000-10,000 |
| 08 | What Price Is Worth Buying, What Signal Forces a Sell (series finale) | DCF three scenarios + red-line list + position framework | 10,000-12,000 |

Add a `00-series-overview.md` as a table-of-contents index; don't publish it.

**The outline must fit the company**: the template grew out of a specific complex company; it is not a mold. If an essay has no independent content (e.g., a company with no investment portfolio can't write essay 04), replace it with an axis unique to that company (e.g., a specific product line or AI bet), or fold it into fewer essays per the merge method above. The test: can this essay stand alone around "one sharp question"?

## 2.5. Intra-Essay Structure Template (distilled from an 8-essay reference series)

Each essay is not free-form; it has a fixed skeleton. Shared structure:

- **Header quote block**: `> Understanding X series · Essay 0N · <main axis>`, with reading time on the opening essay
- **The first section is always a hook**: a set of counterintuitive numbers / an anomalous curve / a number the market worries about — no background throat-clearing
- **End of each essay**: `## Key Points Recap` (5-8 bullets, numbers strictly consistent with the body) + `## Next Up` (tease with a sharp question, don't spoil the conclusion)
- **Two italic lines at the end**: `*This is Essay 0N of the Understanding X series…*` + `*This series is based on public information and a value-investing framework and does not constitute investment advice.*`

Per-essay skeletons:

| Essay | Skeleton |
|-------|----------|
| 01 Opening | The historical curve of the market repeatedly "re-characterizing" the stock → three common illusions (break each with earnings data) → a "you need several pairs of glasses" reading map table (linking to 02-08) → a valuation-hook number table (leave suspense, give no conclusion) |
| 02 Moat | Challenger-failure roster (with names and years) → the essence of switching cost (what users can't migrate) → the ecosystem flywheel → will the moat still be there in 5/10 years → a reverse thought experiment ("if I had R$100 bn, could I replicate it") |
| 03 Profit Engine | Counterintuitive number opener → why the core product stays evergreen (dig down to pricing power) → quantify the growth runway (share path + counter-evidence: the story might not work) → organizational capability (why it keeps shipping products) → why the market underrates it → AI/era-variable impact on this business → the picture in 5 years |
| 04 Hidden Assets | TOP-holdings table (**count only the non-consolidated portion**; state consolidated ones separately to avoid double-counting) → accounting-treatment differences (what hits the income statement vs. not) → "won't sell vs. might trim" classification → why the market applies a discount (item by item) → a few surprising findings → a simple method to estimate the discount |
| 05 AI/Era Variable | The number the market worries about → dissect winners/losers business by business (severe divergence, no overall conclusion) → the key counter-question ("X lost one battle, can it still win the war") → 5-10-year "bypassed" scenario modeling (rebut or concede each) → 5 scenarios → a monitoring-signal checklist for the next year |
| 06 Financials | An anomalous curve opener → income statement (attribute gross-margin change) → metric choice (Non-GAAP vs. GAAP, why) → EPS and buybacks → balance sheet (net cash) → cash flow and FCF yield → shareholder returns via the "$1 test" → is the ROE change good or bad → valuation cross-section + historical comparison → run the 10-item value-investing checklist item by item → compress the financials into three sentences |
| 07 Management | Core-team table → the key person's long-term contribution (use specific decisions, not adjectives) → interest alignment (holdings/pay as a share of profit) → **integrity test: pick one real crisis and examine the response** → 5-year promise fulfillment rate (check each against the original words at the time) → capital-allocation discipline → succession concerns (don't dodge) → the "three questions about people" (honest / capable / passionate) → composite score |
| 08 Decision | SOTP sum-of-the-parts valuation (make clear "what you're actually buying") → historical valuation percentile + **must warn "the rear-view mirror is not the GPS"** → future-profit three-scenario table (bull/base/bear + implied growth, labeled "this is a forecast, not a fact") → red-line list (a trigger ≠ a forced sell, but forces a review) → close with odds-based thinking |

## 3. Writing-Style Standard

### Tone

- **Direct, sharp, no filler** — the first sentence gives a number or a counterintuitive conclusion
- **Value-investing framework** — Buffett / Munger / Duan Yongping / Li Lu perspectives woven in (but don't pile up quotes)
- **No preset stance** — lay out the data first, then the logic, then the conclusion
- **Present both sides** — every core judgment carries a "but on the other hand…" counter-view
- **Feed-preview feel** — the first 18-20 words must stand on their own (mobile preview)

### Banned Words

| Banned | Reason | Replacement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| obviously / inevitably / certainly | Subjective absolutism | the data shows / the evidence indicates |
| I think / I feel | Subjective tone | delete, or change to "under this framework" |
| textbook-grade / stroke of genius | Clickbait praise | describe the specific fact |
| severely mismatched / severely underrated | Strong subjective words | give a specific discount percentage |
| perfect / flawless | One-sided judgment | add a counter-observation |

### Headline Style

- Use a **contrast number** or a **counter-consensus conclusion** as the hook ("15 years, 7 challenges all failed", "annual pay of R$X is just 0.0165% of profit")
- Subtitle is neutral and summarizes the content ("— `<essence judgment>`")
- **Avoid clickbait metaphors**: "the little Buffett", "the X of Brazil", trend slang — all avoided
- Use terms professional readers know (prefer "Berkshire" over "Buffett", company names over person names)

## 4. Strict Fact-Check Checklist (Core IP)

### "False-Precision" Traps to Watch Before You Write

1. **Probability-weighted expected value**: `30% × A + 50% × B + 20% × C = expected +X%` — this kind of math is almost all garbage; the probability allocation is purely subjective and gives readers false precision. **Only list scenarios + trigger conditions + direction; do not compute a weighted expectation.**
2. **Third-party MAU/share estimates**: sources like SimilarWeb / Sensor Tower / market-research firms differ hugely in methodology (can differ 2-3× at the same point in time). **Use only the two most credible for an anchor, describe the rest qualitatively.**
3. **Linear extrapolation of historical growth**: `2025 +33% × 5-year CAGR → 2030 X` is financial-illiterate forecasting. **Scenario assumptions + high/low range + not a promise.**
4. **Undisclosed shareholding percentages**: for private/non-listed companies, stakes **are never publicly disclosed**. **Give a range, label it "unknowable".**
5. **Strong attribution**: competitor failed = because of X. List all the multiple causes; **this article does not make single-cause attributions.**

### The 7 Checks That Must Run on Every Revision

```
□ 1. Cross-essay number consistency: total market cap, Non-GAAP net profit, key stakes % aligned across the whole series
□ 2. Metric labeling: which of Non-GAAP / GAAP / adj.-for-SBC / FCF each figure uses, clear throughout
□ 3. Double-count scan: consolidated subsidiaries not in the "investment portfolio", SOTP not double-counted
□ 4. Cross-comparison fairness: no "core-business PE (ex-cash+portfolio)" vs. "peer PE (not adjusted)"
□ 5. Delete all probability weighting: see the point above
□ 6. Soften all absolutist phrasing: grep "obviously|inevitably|severely|textbook|perfect"
□ 7. Third-party data sourcing: every non-filing figure followed by "(source: X)"
```

### Model Preferences

Before writing, **list the known hard-error risks**:
- Historical return multiples: must use the cumulative-invested-capital basis (e.g., a 33× return, not 58×)
- Shareholding percentages: must use the latest filing / market-data source (e.g., a holding company owns 1.5% of a peer, not 6.4%)
- "Dividend-in-kind / asset distribution" accounting: a deemed-disposal gain is recognized on the declaration date under IFRIC 17 (watch which fiscal year it lands in)
- Total share count rebounds: SBC concentrated in early-year grants can push the share count up short-term

## 5. Execution Flow

### Phase 1: Research (complete before writing essays 01-02)

1. Read the company's last 5 years of annual reports and its latest quarterly report (via CVM/RAD and the company's RI site; SEC EDGAR for US-listed ADRs)
2. Read at least 3 independent sell-side research reports (find the consensus + the counter-consensus)
3. Use `/investment-team` or `/investment-research` to first generate an internal research draft
4. Confirm the core arguments of the essays with the user (avoid discovering the direction is wrong only after writing)

### Phase 2: Writing (write in order 01 → final essay, don't skip)

- Save each essay as it's finished to `reports/{Company}/Understanding-{Company}/0X-XX.md`
- **Directory-conflict rule**: if `reports/{Company}/Understanding-{Company}/` already exists (an old series), don't overwrite or mix — write the new series into a date-suffixed new folder `reports/{Company}/Understanding-{Company}-{YYYYMMDD}/`, leaving the old folder intact
- Don't push to GitHub immediately — wait for the user's review
- Revise after the user gives feedback
- Only `git push` after revisions

### Phase 3: Cross-Essay Consistency Scan (after all essays are written)

Dispatch an Explore agent to scan the whole series in parallel for the following:
1. Whether the same number (market cap, net profit, shareholding %) is consistent across essays
2. Whether the same term (e.g., FCF, SBC, Non-GAAP) is explained on first appearance
3. Reference relations: does essay 02's "see essay 06 for detail" actually correspond?
4. Whether the Key Points Recap is numerically consistent with the body

### Phase 4: Final Check Before Publishing

```bash
# Must grep locally before pushing (per ai-berkshire privacy rules)
grep -r "<local username>\|/Users/\|<personal identifying info>" reports/ | head
```

Only after confirming it's clean, run `git pull --rebase && git commit && git push`.

## 6. Revision-Feedback Handling Flow

When the user gives revision feedback, handle it in this order:

### 1. Verify the Fact First (don't just edit)

If the user says "X data is wrong," first use Bash/Read to find the original data and cross-verify:
- Check the earnings/filing reports for the same company in the ai-berkshire project
- Check the official filing (CVM/RAD, company RI site) or the market-data source
- Give a three-way comparison: "the data the user stated vs. the data I found vs. the data I used before"

### 2. Judge the Revision Level

| Level | Type | Handling |
|-------|------|----------|
| 🔥 Hard error | Wrong number, wrong attribution, wrong metric | Must fix, no hesitation |
| ⚠️ Subjectivized | Strong subjective words, absolutism, clickbait metaphor | Soften or delete |
| 🔬 Granularity | Source labeling, metric refinement | Low priority, balance against readability |
| ❓ Unreliable | Third-party estimates differ widely | **Deleting is safer than editing** (per explicit user instruction) |

### 3. Cascade Check After Revision

When you fix one spot, first think "where else references this number/concept." Examples:
- Changed total market cap → cascade-update PE / core-business PE / discount / FCF yield across the whole series
- Changed a shareholding % → update the TOP-10 ranking + historical-holdings table + trim list
- Changed a metric's definition → update the first definition + later references + the Key Points Recap

### 4. Report Immediately After Pushing

```
Push succeeded (commit hash).
Summary of [N] revisions [with table]:
- What was changed
- What was cascade-changed
- What was not changed

Awaiting next instruction.
```

## 7. What This Skill Does NOT Do

- **Doesn't make the investment decision for the reader** — every essay ends with "does not constitute investment advice"
- **Doesn't predict share price** — only gives "scenario + trigger condition"
- **Doesn't compute a probability-weighted "expected annualized return"** — subjective probability allocation misleads readers
- **Doesn't write "big investor X also holds it"** — using someone else's position to back your own judgment is anti-value-investing
- **Doesn't force all 8 essays** — if an essay lacks enough independent content (e.g., unremarkable management), merge it into another or cut the count

## 8. Compliance and Privacy

- All public reports use **only public information** (filings, official disclosures, brokerage research, reputable third-party institutions)
- Don't use any **user personal information** (company code-names, internal IM, undisclosed position information)
- Before pushing, grep for privacy fields: local username / `/Users/` / real names
- Public attribution follows the user's multi-layer identity policy; don't mix identities

## One-Sentence Summary

**The core skill of writing an "Understanding X series" ≠ writing well, but editing strictly** — 89% of long-form finance pieces die from false-precision numbers, subjective weighted expectations, and absolutist phrasing. This skill exists to flag all these pitfalls: avoid them before writing, scrub them out after.
